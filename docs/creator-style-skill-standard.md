# Creator Style Skill Standard

Every creator skill added to the Creator Style Library must be built from the supplied transcripts and pass this standard. The goal is a reusable creative system, not impersonation or a collection of catchphrases.

## Required analysis

- Point of view: what the creator notices, values, challenges, and assumes about the audience.
- Tone system: emotional temperature, confidence, intimacy, irreverence, sincerity, and how those qualities change by beat.
- Language mechanics: sentence shape, vocabulary range, specificity, rhetorical devices, and spoken rhythm.
- Humor mechanics: setup, target, timing, contrast, exaggeration, callbacks, and restraint.
- Story architecture: how ideas open, escalate, turn, prove themselves, and pay off.
- Attention mechanics: curiosity, unanswered questions, resets, stakes, surprise, and pattern changes.
- Explanation style: examples, analogies, evidence, objections, simplification, and depth.
- Visual instincts: what deserves text, demonstration, montage, reaction, graphic treatment, or silence.
- Audience relationship: teacher, friend, investigator, entertainer, critic, participant, or a deliberate blend.
- Negative space: habits the creator avoids, moments they let breathe, and choices that would feel out of character.

## Required skill structure

1. Creative north star in one precise paragraph.
2. Invariant traits that apply regardless of video length.
3. Flexible style controls describing how intensity can change without losing the voice.
4. A step-by-step reasoning workflow for generating an original video in the extracted style system.
5. Separate prompt modules for ideation, hooks, structure, drafting, humor, visual direction, and revision.
6. Anti-imitation guardrails: no copied signature phrases, personal anecdotes, identity claims, or close paraphrases.
7. A self-critique checklist that tests entertainment, clarity, originality, tonal accuracy, and payoff.
8. Source notes connecting every major conclusion to repeated evidence across the supplied transcripts.

## Quality bar

- Never reduce a creator to adjectives such as “fast,” “funny,” or “high energy.” Explain the mechanism.
- Never hardcode short-form or long-form durations. Describe pacing as relationships between beats.
- Distinguish stable style traits from topic-specific habits found in only one transcript.
- Prefer falsifiable guidance: state what to do, when to do it, and what failure looks like.
- Preserve the user’s subject matter, opinions, facts, and identity. The skill shapes delivery, not authorship.
- The complete skill must work alone after copying, without requiring hidden context from MotionLayer.
